The busiest NIC's bytes fall by the post's factor, about G, and that NIC sets the time. Total bytes on the fabric
barely change: a flat ring pushes everything through the N NICs at node boundaries, while the hierarchical
version spreads nearly the same total over all G x N NICs, one rail each. So 'G times less fabric traffic' is
true per NIC, not for the fabric as a whole.

Flat ring on 4 x 4: every round waits for the network hop (1.35 ms). An NVLink hop needs
0.15 ms of it, so the 12 NVLink hops are busy 11% of each round.

Nuance 12: the two factors are separate. Same 4 x 8 machine, but the in-node links made as slow as the network:
busiest NIC bytes still cut 10.33x; time 41.8 ms flat vs 41.7 ms hierarchical.
The per-NIC traffic cut holds whatever the link speeds, as the post says, but on its own it buys almost no time:
the in-node phases now move 2(G-1)/G n over slow links. The speed-up needs both factors at once.
