With Adam every parameter moves by about lr per coordinate, so ||Delta theta|| grows with the parameter count
and the question is how much of that reaches the output. DeepNet's bound says vanilla Post-LN passes it
through roughly in proportion to the number of sublayers, while DeepNorm's alpha and beta keep it flat.
Measured: depth grows 16x. Post-LN's update grows 22.3x, about linear in depth. At L = 48 one step
moves the output by 11.6, more than the output's own norm of 8: a single step rewrites the function.
DeepNorm's update is 7x smaller at L = 3 and grows 3.0x: much flatter, not exactly
flat at this scale. Pre-LN's final LN bounds its output, so its growth slows at depth.
This measures the bound's quantity on a tiny model; it is not DeepNet's 1,000-layer experiment.
