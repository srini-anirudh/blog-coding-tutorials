Ordered phase: c* = 1, every input maps to the same vector. Chaotic phase: c* < 1, nearby inputs decorrelate.
When c* = 1 the slope C'(c*) equals chi_1, so the post's 1/|ln chi_1| is the depth scale there. In the chaotic
phase the slope at c* < 1 is a different number, so 1/|ln chi_1| is only the ordered-side formula.
The fit matches theory within about 5% in the ordered phase. In the chaotic phase it comes out 1.6 to 1.8
times longer: the map is far from linear over the fitted range and width 1000 adds noise near c*.

Gaussian weights: the mean of s^2 stays of order 1 at criticality, but the spread grows with depth.
Orthogonal + tanh near the origin keeps the spectrum concentrated. Orthogonal + ReLU does not: the 0/1
derivative mask zeroes about half the directions at every layer (most s are below 1e-15), which is
Pennington et al.'s point that ReLU cannot be isometric. 'critical' row: xi is large but finite only because
the bisection lands a hair off the line; the fit over 40 layers cannot see a depth scale that long.
