Part 1. Measured FLOPs per token, one forward + one backward pass, sequence 256.
P = matmul weights (blocks + tied LM head). Attention scores/values are counted separately.

model              P   fwd/P   bwd/P  (fwd+bwd)/P attn fwd / 4LdS attn bwd / fwd attn share
d64-L2       368,640   2.000   4.000        6.000           1.000          2.50x      17.2%
d128-L4    1,327,104   2.000   4.000        6.000           1.000          2.50x      18.7%
d256-L6    5,865,472   2.000   4.000        6.000           1.000          2.50x      13.5%
d512-L10  33,718,272   2.000   4.000        6.000           1.000          2.50x       8.3%

The linear part is exactly 2 FLOPs per weight forward and 4 backward: 6 per weight per token.
Attention is a separate, sequence-length cost. Its backward counts 2.5x its forward here, because
PyTorch's fused attention backward recomputes the score matrix QK^T instead of storing it.
RMSNorm, SiLU, softmax and residual adds are not matmuls and FlopCounterMode does not count them.

Part 2. FLOPs per token under three published conventions, relative to the reference count
(reference = 72 L d^2 + 6 V d + 6 L d S: linear layers, untied head, causal attention).
L = 32, V = 100,352, S = 8,192, d_ffn = 4d, untied embeddings: the post's Figure 3 defaults.

At d = 4096 the conventions agree within about 30%. As d shrinks, the head and attention, which
6N_non-embedding ignores, become most of the work, so Kaplan's count undershoots more and more.
Attention / linear FLOPs at d = 4096, S = 8192: unmasked 12dS/72d^2 = 0.333 (the post's S/(6d)), causal 6dS/72d^2 = 0.167.
Note: FlopCounterMode counts attention unmasked (the full S x S score matrix), as DeepSeek's M does.
