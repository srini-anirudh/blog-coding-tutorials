## A length-4096 dot product of BF16 values in [0, 1). Relative error against float64

------------------+---------------

Adding 1.0 to a BF16 sum 4096 times gives 256: past 256 the gap between BF16 values is 2.

## Adam, lr 3e-4, fitting a 64 x 64 linear layer whose weights start at 1.0 and should end within 1% of it
recipe    | loss @1  | @100      | @300      | @600     
----------+----------+-----------+-----------+----------
fp32      | 0.006332 | 5.62e-05  | 2.148e-08 | 5.106e-12
master    | 0.00672  | 0.0007504 | 0.0007655 | 0.0007635
pure_bf16 | 0.00672  | 0.00672   | 0.00672   | 0.00672  
pure_bf16: BF16 weights and BF16 Adam state. Every update rounds away, so the loss never changes.
master: BF16 compute copy, FP32 master. It learns, then floors where the BF16 copy cannot express the target (weights 1 +- 0.01 sit on a grid of 2^-7 = 0.0078). The FP32 run has no floor.
