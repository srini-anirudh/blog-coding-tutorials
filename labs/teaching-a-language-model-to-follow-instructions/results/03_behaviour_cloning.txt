   Reading. Behaviour cloning is a coin flip per seed: one seed's policy never leaves the ring, another leaves
   within 25 steps. Averaged over seeds the cost grows faster than linearly in T for most of the range (local
   slopes 1.0 to 1.9, noisy with 5 seeds), and it cannot keep the slope of 2 because cost is capped at T. The
   data never showed the way back to the ring, so a rollout that leaves stays out. Recovery data fixes it here:
   executing noisy actions while labelling with the clean expert action (the DART idea) gave zero cost at every
   horizon, and DAgger (label the states the learner visits, refit on the aggregate) took the mean cost to at most
   1.4 steps of 400 by iteration 2 to 4. It is not monotone in the iteration (1 iteration was still bad, 3 was
   worse than 2) because every iteration re-fits from scratch. The slope of the cost is not a clean test of the
   bound: the O(eps T^2) result is worst case, and this is one small task.
