C. Which mistakes does each direction charge for? D has 3 behaviours (0.6, 0.3, 0.1), p varies
   p                                              KL(D||p)   KL(p||D)
   p = D (reference)                                 0.000      0.000
   p puts 0.20 on a behaviour D never shows          0.223        inf
   p puts 0.01 on a behaviour D shows 10%            0.230        inf
   p drops the 10% behaviour entirely                  inf      0.105
   (inf = the reverse direction is unbounded when p puts mass where D has none.)

D. The same loss on two kinds of text, 200 documents each; mean NLL per token in nats (bf16 autocast)
   model                    web text  chat, every token   chat, assistant tokens
   SmolLM2-135M (base)         3.139              4.151                    2.349
   SmolLM2-135M-Instruct       3.279              2.722                    1.630
   Same function, same tokenizer, different text. The base model is better on web text; the instruct model is
   far better on assistant tokens. The instruct model also gives up some web-text loss. One model, one loss,
   and what it is good at is set by the distribution it was last trained on.

E. SFT on a mixture: 800 Alpaca examples, 80% of replies end with 'Hope this helps.' (never asked for), 20% do not.
   Evaluate on 200 held-out prompts: share of replies ending with the sign-off. 3 epochs, lr 2e-5.
   seed 0: share in training data 0.806; share of replies with the sign-off, greedy 0.994, sampled 0.609
   seed 1: share in training data 0.785; share of replies with the sign-off, greedy 0.989, sampled 0.657
   seed 2: share in training data 0.802; share of replies with the sign-off, greedy 1.000, sampled 0.756
   mean over 3 seeds: data 0.798, greedy 0.994, sampled 0.674
   (Replies that hit the 160-token budget without ending are left out of the shares.)
   Greedy decoding picks the dominant mode (about 99% sign-off). Sampling keeps the minority style alive, and more
   of it than the data has: a quarter to two-fifths of sampled replies lack the sign-off, against 20% in the data.
   So the match with the mixture is rough, not calibrated: a 135M model sampled at T=1 also drifts for other reasons,
   and 3 epochs may not have fitted the 80/20 split. What the numbers support is the post's point in miniature:
   the loss keeps both modes, and nothing in it says which one is the unwanted one.
